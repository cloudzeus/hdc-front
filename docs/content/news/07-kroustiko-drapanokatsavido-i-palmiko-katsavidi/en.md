**Short answer:** A percussion drill is built for drilling: it has a chuck, a clutch for controlled screwdriving and a hammer mode for masonry. An impact driver is built for driving: with a 1/4" hex holder and impacts delivered in the direction of rotation, it produces high torque from a small body. Most professionals keep both.

On the counter they look alike: the same pistol shape, the same battery, and both of them "hit". On the job, however, they do different things. Choose the wrong one and you will be drilling with one tool and wrestling screws with the other.

## What is the difference between a percussion drill and an impact driver?

The difference lies in how each tool "hits" and in what it holds at the front.

### Percussion drill: chuck, clutch, hammer action
It holds drill bits and screwdriver bits in a chuck, usually 13 mm. The clutch stops driving at an adjustable torque, so you do not damage screws or material. In hammer mode the bit receives small blows forwards, into the material, which lets it advance through brick and masonry.

### Impact driver: hex holder and impacts in the direction of rotation
It holds bits with a 1/4" hex shank in a hex holder. When the screw resists, an internal mechanism delivers impacts in the direction of rotation. Torque is delivered in blows, so the tool twists less against your wrist.

In short: the percussion drill strikes forwards, the impact driver strikes around.

## When does the percussion drill win?

When the job is mostly holes. According to Milwaukee, the [M18 FPD3-502X](/en/proion/kroustiko-drapanokatsavido-m18fpd3-502x-fuel-4933479860-4933479860) drills up to 89 mm in wood and 16 mm in steel and masonry. Choose the drill when you:

- bore service holes with hole saws or wood bits,
- set anchors in brick and masonry,
- need precise screwdriving with a clutch on furniture and hinges.

AUTOSTOP on the FPD3 shuts the tool down when the bit binds and the body starts to twist sharply. For continuous drilling in concrete, however, the right tool is an SDS-Plus rotary hammer.

## When does the impact driver win?

When the job is mostly screws, especially many, long ones or screws into hard material. According to Milwaukee, the [M18 FID3-502X](/en/proion/palmiko-katsavidi-1-4-m18-fid3-502x-4933479865-4933479865) delivers 226 Nm from a body just 113 mm long and tightens bolts up to M16. It suits:

- timber framing, roofs and pergolas,
- fixing to steel framing and sheet metal,
- repetitive driving all day long, where hand fatigue matters.

DRIVE CONTROL on the FID3 has four modes: precision, protection of fastener and material, maximum performance and one that adjusts automatically. That way the same tool handles both delicate work and heavy screws.

## What is the SURGE hydraulic impact driver?

The [M18 FQID-0X SURGE](/en/proion/katsavidi-ydrayliko-palmiko-1-4-m18-fqid-0x-fuel-4933459187-4933459187) is an impact driver with a FLUID-DRIVE hydraulic powertrain. It reduces metal-on-metal contact in the impact mechanism. According to Milwaukee:

- it is the company's quietest impact driver, about 50% quieter than standard ones,
- it produces two times less noise and vibration,
- it matches standard brushless impact drivers for fastening into wood,
- it reaches up to 3,000 rpm and 4,000 ipm and tightens bolts up to M14.

It is a sensible choice for indoor work, occupied buildings and long days of driving. There is also an M12 version, the M12 FQID.

## Why do so many people buy both?

Because a job is rarely only holes or only screws. With two tools you drill the pilot hole with the drill and drive with the impact driver, without constantly swapping bits. Since both take the same M18 battery, a second tool does not mean a second system. That is why there are ready-made [percussion drill and impact driver kits](/en/katalogos/set-me-kroustiko-drapanokatsavido-kai-palmiko) with batteries and a charger.

## Are there M12 equivalents?

Yes, for those who want a smaller, lighter pair. The M12 FPD2 delivers 45 Nm and has a 13 mm metal chuck, 13 clutch settings plus drilling and hammer modes. The M12 FID2 delivers 170 Nm with a length of 127 mm and 4-mode DRIVE CONTROL, including a self-tapping screw mode.

## Where does an impact wrench fit in?

It is the third tool in the family. An impact driver can handle bolts too, but for heavy fastening on steelwork, machinery and vehicles there are [1/2 impact wrenches](/en/katalogos/boulonokleida-1-2-3), which take sockets instead of bits.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I drill with an impact driver?
For light holes, yes, using drill bits with a hex shank. An impact driver has no chuck, clutch or hammer mode, though, so for masonry, large diameters and clean precision holes the percussion drill is the right tool.

### Can I drive screws with a percussion drill?
Yes. In screwdriving mode the clutch stops the screw exactly where you want, which is useful on furniture, hinges and soft materials. For lots of long screws, however, an impact driver is easier on your hand and gets the job done more comfortably.

### What do rpm, ipm and bpm mean?
Rpm is revolutions per minute. Ipm is impacts per minute on an impact driver, which strikes in the direction of rotation. Bpm is blows per minute on a percussion drill, which strikes forwards, into the material.

### Is SURGE weaker than a standard impact driver?
According to Milwaukee, the M18 FQID SURGE matches standard brushless impact drivers for fastening into wood, while being about 50% quieter. For bolts, Milwaukee rates it up to M14, compared with M16 for the M18 FID3.

### Do I need special bits for an impact driver?
It is best to use bits designed for impact drivers, such as Milwaukee's SHOCKWAVE IMPACT DUTY range. They have a laser-hardened WEAR GUARD tip and a SHOCK ZONE that absorbs impact peaks, so they break less often.
