**Short answer:** M12 is Milwaukee's 12 V sub-compact platform, built for tight spaces, overhead work and a light tool you can use all day. M18 is the 18 V platform for heavy drilling, cutting and fastening. Batteries do not cross over between the two, but a single M12-18 charger charges both.

"M12 or M18?" is one of the first questions anyone asks when putting together a Milwaukee kit. The right answer is rarely "just one". It depends on what you do most hours of the day, where you work and how hard the tool is pushed. Below we put the two platforms side by side, using figures from Milwaukee's official catalogue.

## What is the difference between M12 and M18?

The first difference is voltage: 12 V against 18 V. Everything else follows from that.

- **M12:** a sub-compact platform with more than 125 solutions, according to Milwaukee, which describes it as the largest sub-compact cordless system. The priorities are small size, low weight and access.
- **M18:** an 18 V platform with more than 325 tools and solutions, from drills and impact drivers to grinders, rotary hammers and saws. The priority is power.

The second thing to know concerns batteries. Every M18 battery fits every M18 tool, and all M12 REDLITHIUM batteries work with all current M12 tools. The two platforms do not swap batteries, though: each tool takes the packs of its own platform.

Where they meet is the charger. The M12-18 C and M12-18 FC dual-platform chargers charge both M12 and M18 batteries. Together the two systems open up hundreds of combined solutions with just one charger in the van.

## What are M12 FUEL and M18 FUEL?

Both platforms have their own FUEL line, the top tier for performance. A FUEL tool combines three elements that Milwaukee develops in-house:

1. **A POWERSTATE brushless motor**, designed and built by Milwaukee for its own tools, aiming for more speed and torque from a smaller body. With no carbon brushes it needs no brush maintenance, runs quieter and cools down faster.
2. **A REDLITHIUM battery**, the company's battery technology.
3. **REDLINK PLUS electronics**, which coordinate tool and battery and protect both from overload.

So the fair comparison is not "basic M12 against M18 FUEL", but M12 FUEL against M18 FUEL, in the same type of tool.

## How do the drills and impact drivers compare on paper?

We took the two tools that go into every case first: the percussion drill and the 1/4" impact driver, in M12 FUEL and M18 FUEL versions.

| Model | Type | Max torque | Body length | Speed | Capacity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| M12 FPD2 | percussion drill | 45 Nm | 152 mm | 0–450 / 0–1,550 rpm | wood 35 mm, steel 13 mm, masonry 13 mm |
| M18 FPD3 | percussion drill | 158 Nm | 175 mm | 0–500 / 0–2,100 rpm | wood 89 mm, steel 16 mm, masonry 16 mm |
| M12 FID2 | 1/4" impact driver | 170 Nm | 127 mm | up to 3,600 rpm, 4,000 ipm | bolts up to M14 |
| M18 FID3 | 1/4" impact driver | 226 Nm | 113 mm | up to 3,900 rpm, 4,400 ipm | bolts up to M16 |

*Data from Milwaukee Europe product pages (M12 FPD2, M18 FPD3, M12 FID2, M18 FID3).*

On the drills the gap is large. The M18 FPD3 has more than three times the torque and bores 89 mm holes in wood, against 35 mm for the M12 FPD2, while being only 23 mm longer. For hole saws, wood spade and auger bits, and holes in brick above 13 mm, M18 is the tool.

With impact drivers the picture changes. The M12 FID2, at 170 Nm, comfortably handles wood screws, self-tapping screws and bolts up to M14. And here is a surprise: according to Milwaukee's pages, the M18 FID3 (113 mm) is shorter than the M12 FID2 (127 mm). So "M12 always means smaller" does not hold for every tool. Overall size and weight in the hand also depend on the battery you fit, so it is worth holding both before you decide.

You will find more models among the [impact drivers](/en/katalogos/palmika-katsavidia) and [cordless percussion drills](/en/katalogos/kroustika-drapana-2) in our catalogue.

## When is M12 the better choice?

- When you spend long hours working overhead or at arm's length.
- When you work inside cabinets, distribution boards, suspended ceilings and service shafts.
- When most holes are small and screws are medium-sized.
- When you want a second, light kit that fits in a bag rather than a case.

## When do you need M18?

- When you often drill large diameters in wood or metal.
- When you need tools that M12 does not cover as comfortably, such as SDS-Plus rotary hammers and angle grinders.
- When the tool runs under load all the time and you want power in reserve.

## Which platform suits each trade?

The suggestions below are practical pointers, not rules:

- **Electricians:** M12 FUEL for the day, with a drill driver and impact driver for back boxes, cable trays and boards. One M18 for through-holes in timber and for the rotary hammer in concrete.
- **Plumbers:** M12 for brackets and tight shafts. M18 for hole saws and large pipe penetrations.
- **HVAC and refrigeration technicians:** M12 is handy on the ladder and for outdoor-unit brackets. M18 comes in for heavy drilling through walls.
- **Carpenters and joiners:** M18 for the main drill and impact driver, for the torque on long screws. An M12 for furniture assembly and fine work.
- **Fitters and metalworkers:** M18 as the base, since steel diameters and bolt sizes climb quickly. M12 stays for adjustments and spots with no room.

## Why start with M12 and add M18?

For many tradespeople the most sensible route is to start with one of the [M12 combo kits](/en/katalogos/set-combo-ergaleion-12v), usually a drill driver and an impact driver, which cover most everyday work. When the need for more power appears, they add M18 tools or one of the [M18 combo kits](/en/katalogos/set-combo-ergaleion-18v).

## Frequently asked questions

### Does an M12 battery fit an M18 tool?
No. Each tool takes the batteries of its own platform: M12 batteries go in M12 tools and M18 batteries in M18 tools. What the two platforms share is the charger, since the M12-18 C and M12-18 FC charge both.

### Is M12 enough for professional use?
For many jobs, yes. According to Milwaukee, the M12 FID2 impact driver delivers 170 Nm and drives bolts up to M14. For large diameters in wood, for concrete and for continuous heavy use, M18 is the safer choice.

### How many tools does each platform have?
According to Milwaukee, the M12 platform has more than 125 solutions and the M18 platform more than 325. Milwaukee describes M12 as the largest sub-compact cordless system.

### Is an M12 tool always smaller than its M18 equivalent?
Not always. According to Milwaukee's product pages, the M18 FID3 impact driver is 113 mm long, while the M12 FID2 is 127 mm. With the drills it is the other way round: the M12 FPD2 is 152 mm and the M18 FPD3 175 mm.

### Do I need two chargers if I own both M12 and M18 tools?
Not necessarily. The M12-18 C and M12-18 FC dual-platform chargers accept both M12 and M18 batteries, so one charger covers all your kit.
