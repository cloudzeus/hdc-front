**Short answer:** Choose a Milwaukee laser by the job. A cross line laser (CLL, M12 CLL4P) for tiling, kitchens and quick layout. A 3-plane 360° laser (M12 3PL) for drywall and suspended ceilings. A rotary laser (M18 RLOHVG300) for outdoor work and long distances. All the models we looked at use a green beam and are Class 2.

The laser level has become a core tool for tilers, electricians, drywallers and contractors. There are many options, though, and the model names look alike. Below we explain the types, the figures worth reading on the spec sheet and which model suits which job.

## What types of laser does Milwaukee make?

### Cross line lasers

They project one horizontal and one vertical line. This is the simplest and quickest option for a room or a wall. The group includes the alkaline-powered CLL, the M12 CLL4P, which also adds 4 points, and the M12 CLLP you will find in our catalogue.

### 3-plane 360° lasers

The M12 3PL projects one horizontal and two vertical 360° planes. From a single set-up it covers the whole room with lines: walls, floor and ceiling. There is also an auto-alignment version, the M12 A3PLO.

### Rotary lasers

The head rotates and creates a reference plane over a long distance, usually read with a detector. The M18 RLOHVG300 works horizontally and vertically. Our catalogue also lists the M18 RLOH600.

### Distance meters

They are not levels, but they measure distance with a laser beam. If that is what you need, see our [distance meters](/en/katalogos/metrites-apostaseon-anichneytes-metallon), such as the [LDM 45](/en/proion/apostasiometro-laser-ldm-45-4933459277-4933459277).

## Green or red beam: which is easier to see?

According to Milwaukee, a green beam offers up to 4x better visibility than a red one. The difference shows mainly in bright rooms, on white walls with daylight from a window and over longer distances indoors. All four models in the table below are green.

## Visible range vs detector range: what do they mean?

**Visible range** is the distance at which you can see the line with the naked eye. **Detector range** is the distance at which a laser receiver picks up the beam, even when you cannot see it.

- CLL: 30 m visible, 50 m with a detector.
- M12 CLL4P and M12 3PL: 38 m visible, up to 100 m with a detector.
- M18 RLOHVG300: 300 m working range with the RD300G detector.

Outdoors in sunshine the line fades fast, so a receiver is essential there. You will find detectors among our [line and rotary lasers](/en/katalogos/grammika-peristrofika-laser-dektes-laser).

## How accurate is a Milwaukee laser?

Milwaukee lists 0.3 mm/m accuracy for the M12 CLL4P and M12 3PL. The error is given per metre, so it grows the further you are from the laser. For the M18 RLOHVG300 rotary laser, accuracy is given as ±1.6 mm at 30 m.

Also check the **self-levelling range**. The CLL, M12 CLL4P and M12 3PL self-level within 4° (the 3PL in 3 seconds), the M18 RLOHVG300 within ±5°. The M12 CLL4P also has an out-of-level warning and a pendulum lock for transport.

## What do IP54, IP66 and drop resistance mean?

The IP rating shows how well the tool is protected against dust and water. The CLL, M12 CLL4P and M12 3PL are IP54, meaning they withstand dust and splashes. The M18 RLOHVG300 is IP66, suited to outdoor work. According to Milwaukee, the first three survive a 1 m drop and the rotary laser a 1.5 m drop.

## M12 battery or AA batteries?

The CLL runs on 4 AA batteries, and Milwaukee lists more than 8 hours of run-time. AA batteries are available everywhere, but they need replacing.

The M12 models take the same battery as your other M12 tools: more than 9 hours for the M12 CLL4P on an M12 B3 and more than 15 hours for the M12 3PL on an M12 B4. The M18 RLOHVG300 exceeds 32 hours on an M18 B5. If you already own M12 or M18 tools, the choice is probably easy.

## What does "Class 2 laser" mean and how do you use one safely?

Class 2 covers low-power visible-beam lasers. With brief exposure the eye's blink reflex normally protects you. That does not make the laser a toy:

- never stare into the beam,
- never point the laser at other people's eyes, whether colleagues or passers-by,
- when the line runs at eye level, warn everyone working in the area,
- keep the tool away from children.

## Which Milwaukee laser for which job?

- **Tiling, kitchens, cabinets:** a cross line laser, the CLL or the M12 CLL4P. The floor-to-ceiling points on the CLL4P help you transfer a point vertically.
- **Drywall and suspended ceilings:** the M12 3PL, because one set-up gives you lines on every wall and on the ceiling. It also comes as a PACKOUT kit, the [M12 3PLKIT-401P](/en/proion/laser-aytorythmizomeno-comp-m12-3plkit-401-p-4933478960-4933478960).

## Frequently asked questions

### Why are green lasers preferred over red ones?
According to Milwaukee, a green beam offers up to 4x better visibility than a red one. In practice the line reads more clearly, especially in bright rooms. All the models compared in this article use a green beam.

### Is a Class 2 laser dangerous?
Class 2 covers low-power visible-beam lasers. With brief exposure the eye's blink reflex normally protects you. That is not a licence, though: never stare into the beam and never point it at people.

### Do I need a laser detector?
Only when you cannot see the line with the naked eye, over long distances or in strong light. According to Milwaukee, the M12 CLL4P and M12 3PL reach 38 m visible and up to 100 m with a detector, while the M18 RLOHVG300 works up to 300 m with the RD300G detector.

### What is the difference between the CLL and the M12 CLL4P?
The CLL runs on 4 AA alkaline batteries and projects a cross line, with 30 m visible range and 50 m with a detector. The M12 CLL4P runs on an M12 battery, adds 4 points (floor-to-ceiling plumb and horizontal) and reaches 38 m visible and up to 100 m with a detector, with 0.3 mm/m accuracy.

### What does a 4° self-levelling range mean?
It means the laser levels itself when you set it on a surface tilted by up to 4°. On a steeper tilt it cannot self-level. The M12 CLL4P warns you when it is out of level.
